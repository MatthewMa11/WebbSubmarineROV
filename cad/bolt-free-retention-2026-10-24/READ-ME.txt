R7 EXTERNAL COLLAR THRUSTER - PROTOTYPE

Purpose
The four original solid drive keys match the reference rotor's four openings.
They transmit torque. The split sleeve and threaded collar grip the outside of
the rotating motor bell to resist axial separation. The jam ring is tightened
against the collar to resist the collar backing off. There is no internal
spacer and no external clamp bolt, nut, or washer in this attachment.

This is a new attachment method. It does not use the old four propeller screws.
The frame, cap, motor-base screws and stationary mounting hardware are retained.

Print and test the small sleeve coupon plus collar and jam ring first, on the
same P1S, PLA Basic, orientation, layer height and extrusion settings intended
for the full part. Print at 100% scale in millimetres. Thread axis should be
vertical; keep support material off the threads and clean the lead-ins. Start
with 0.16 mm layers and at least five walls. Confirm in the slicer that the
thread crests and collet fingers have continuous extrusion paths. Do not use
scaling to correct a tight thread because it changes the motor pattern too.
The new files in the print folder have the axis vertical and their base at
Z=0. The fan has its broad front hub face down. The test coupon has a shorter
connecting back ring and tests thread/bore fit only; its flex and grip strength
do not represent the full fan. Do not install the coupon in the thruster.

Assembly
1. With the motor unpowered and outside the frame, confirm the bare motor
   rotates freely. Leave it outside while fitting and locking the fan so both
   rings are accessible.
2. From the smaller, free rear end of the propeller sleeve, thread on the main
   collar FIRST and advance it toward the blades. Then thread on the thin jam
   ring so it sits behind the collar. The rings cannot pass one another. Keep
   both loose while fitting the fan over the bell.
3. Seat all four drive keys evenly in the reference rotor openings. The fan
   must sit squarely and fully against its locating interface. The keys are
   not screw threads; do not force screws into them or reinstall the spacer.
4. Hand-tighten the main collar until the sleeve grips the bell and the fan
   cannot be pulled off or rocked. Hold the collar and counter-tighten the
   jam ring against it. Do not drive the collar hard against its travel stop.
   If the fan still slips or rocks, stop and correct the fit; more force may
   crack the printed sleeve or distort the motor bell.
5. With the front cap removed, insert the locked motor/fan unit through the
   front opening and seat the motor base in the existing centering cup. Install
   the four motor-base screws through the rear access pockets and tighten them
   evenly without bottoming in the motor. Fit the unchanged cap and its existing
   through-bolts. Route wires away from all rotating parts. Turn the fan through
   several full revolutions by hand and check every blade tip, not one angle.

Physical acceptance before powered operation
The printed fit, actual bell diameter, PLA creep, pull-off force, motor heating,
balance, fatigue and operating speed have not been measured or certified by
CAD. Reject the print if it cracks, rubs, has rough or distorted blades, or
loses its grip. Check axial pull and rotational slip by hand on the real motor,
mark the two rings to observe loosening, and reassess after initial testing.
A clear CAD assembly is not a guarantee of a secure physical joint or thrust.

STEP limitation: two tiny blade-tip sliver faces in the propeller and the
cap/nozzle import-validity flags are inherited from the unchanged baseline.
The new sleeve faces and both rings pass the local B-rep checks, and delivered
STLs are closed manifold meshes. The complete legacy STEP is not advertised as
globally kernel-valid.

Nozzle-R6p1-flat-motor-boss.stl and Front-cap-R4-unchanged.stl are existing parts;
if you already have them, the R7 coupling itself does not require reprinting
those two parts. The small sleeve coupon is a test piece, not an assembly part.
The existing-parts-unchanged subfolder contains the original frame/cap meshes
with their original orientation; use the successful settings from your print.
