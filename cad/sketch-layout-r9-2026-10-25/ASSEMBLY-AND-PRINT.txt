LAYOUT
From the front: fixed cap, rotating fan, motor, fixed rear motor support.
The existing frame surrounds them. Frame, cap, motor and stationary fastener
geometry/placement are preserved. The fan no longer has a deep cup wrapped
around the motor bell. The existing outer blade profiles move8.0 mm forward
and meet a new flat R20 hub with rounded blade roots. The main hub's forward
face is atY19.3. The full outer blade profile is retained beyondR27.01;
their plane sits ahead of motor faceY14. The pitch gives the blades axial
depth; leading edges may lie alongside the motor face but clear its body
radially. Inner roots taper into the hub, keeping the cap's center clear.

AXLE ATTACHMENT
The four existing solid5.5 mm keys on the18 mm pattern carry torque from
the matching motor face. The two-jaw M3 axle clamp retains the fan axially.
No motor insert/spacer, external compression collar or jam ring is used.
The cap stays attached to the frame and does not rotate with the fan.

NEW CLAMP HARDWARE
2 x M3x16 socket cap screws (5.68 mm nominal head diameter,3 mm head height).
2 x ordinary M3 nuts (5.5 mm across flats,2.4 mm thick).
Install the two screws in opposite directions, as modeled. Broad printed
pad faces support the hardware without washers. Each screw has about1.2 mm
nominal protrusion beyond the nut. The8.6 mm access wells provide local CAD
clearance for an8.4 mm OD hollow nut driver. Tighten the clamp outside the
frame; the frame obstructs a long straight driver after installation.
Check your actual tool. Reuse the existing motor-base/frame/cap hardware
in the order below.

INSTALLATION
1.Work on the bench with the motor outside the frame. Remove the front cap
  from the frame and withdraw its four motor-base screws and washers so
  their tips do not protrude into the motor seat.
2.With the removable upper jaw off, slide the fan onto the projecting shaft
  and align all four drive keys with their matching motor-face openings.
  Seat all keys evenly. Do not force a key into a mismatched opening.
3.Put the upper jaw in its matching rectangular seat. Its larger pilot
  relief faces the motor; its smaller shaft groove faces the shaft tip/cap.
4.Still outside the frame, fit the twoM3x16 bolts in opposite directions and
  add the twoM3 nuts to the outer bearing faces. Tighten alternately in small
  increments. A long driver can approach these nuts before frame insertion.
5.The fan should resist a firm hand pull and twist while a small jaw gap
  remains. Stop if the jaws bottom out before gripping or if plastic bends
  or cracks; that printed fit needs correction, not additional tightening.
6.Hold the motor's fixed base and turn the fan by hand through a complete
  turn. Check retention again. Keep the wires clear of the rotating blades.
7.Insert the assembled fan and motor through the frame's open front, keeping
  their axis centered and the motor-base screw pattern aligned. Feed the
  wires through the existing route and seat the motor gently. Do not force
  it or pinch the wires. The cap is still off during this step.
8.Install the four existing motor-base screws with their washers through
  the rear access pockets and tighten evenly into the motor's fixed base.
  Turn the fan by hand again. Then refit the unchanged cap using its existing
  through-bolts/nuts and repeat the complete hand turn. Nothing should rub.

TEST PRINT / P1S PLA BASIC
Print TEST-FIRST-R9-lower-jaw.stl together with the upper jaw first, using
the same profile intended for the fan. It duplicates the actual shaft bore
and clamp bolt positions without printing the complete fan. It does not
test blade roots, drive-key engagement, dynamic balance or powered thrust.

All STL files are millimetres. Import at100% without scaling.
PRINT folder files have been oriented and placed onZ=0:
-Fan: forward side down; motor axis vertical. The pitched blades project
 slightly ahead of the hub, so use supports below the hub and blade
 overhangs. Remove support residue without damaging the blades.
-Upper jaw: shaft-tip-facing flat end down; its axle axis vertical.
-Small lower-jaw test coupon: flat lower bearing face down as supplied.
-Frame/cap: unchanged copies; use the previous printing orientations.
Starting profile:0.16 mm layers,6walls, upper jaw100% infill. Validate these
settings with the test print. They are not a tested production profile.

PHYSICAL LIMITS
CAD checks:46bodyassembly,7partrotatinggroup, nativeNoInterferences and
completed360degreeanimation. Continuous conservative sweep clears the cap
by0.699 mm, frame by3.249 mm and all stationaryfasteners by5.43 mm.
Outer blade STL surfaces agree with the reference after8 mm translation
within0.02 mm at107,278sampledpoints (max0.016965 mm). This is mesh-surface
fidelity, not a physical printer accuracy claim. Old invalid STEP tip faces
make scalar mass/Boolean profile comparisons unreliable; those diagnostics
are retained in the verification report. The complete final fan B-rep is valid.

Reference shaft diameter3.175 mm; nominal printed clamp bore3.30 mm.
Straight shaft contact length3.05 mm, Y16.25..19.30; joint gap0.30 mm.
Nominal geometric closing travel about0.125 mm. Pilot relief diameter9.6 mm
for the9 mm reference motor pilot. Actual printing/motor tolerances matter.
The cap has a3.5 mm reference shaft passage; check it is free on the actual
shaft after printing. Do not treat the cap as a shaft bearing.

This is a fit-test prototype. Rigid CAD checks cannot establish clamp force,
pull-off capacity, key/root strength, tightening torque, fatigue, PLA creep,
nut loosening, lifetime, safe speed or powered balance. Validate fit and
retention physically and recheck after the first controlled run.
