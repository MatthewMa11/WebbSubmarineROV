PERMANENT MOTOR CENTERING COVER - R3
17 October 2026

WHAT TO PRINT
Nozzle-centering-cup-R3.stl replaces the R2 nozzle. The centering cup is part of
the nozzle, so there is no separate cover, clip or extra fastener to install.
Keep the R2 71 mm propeller and 22.6 mm spacer, original gray front cover and
existing hardware. The complete package includes these unchanged STL files.
All STLs use millimetres at 100% scale and retain the native assembly axes.

HOW IT HELPS ASSEMBLY
A fixed centering cup now partially covers the exposed stationary motor base.
Its tapered entrance guides the motor base toward the existing mounting face
while the four motor-base screws are being aligned. Four rounded cable windows
between the support sectors let the three motor wires exit toward the rear ring.
The slots intentionally leave openings for wiring and water circulation.

The guide has a 37 mm outside diameter, 28.5 mm nominal locator throat, and a
33.5 mm nominal entrance diameter. The entrance edges are rounded 0.35 mm and
the cable-window edges 0.25 mm. The new cup's forward end is at Y=-6.25 mm.
The four mounting screws still provide the motor's final location and retention.
The guide is an assembly aid; it is not a rotor bearing or a waterproof seal.

CAD VERIFICATION
The nozzle and cup are one closed, connected native STL body. All 34 mesh and
native geometry checks pass. The reference rotating hub clears the added cup
by 3.25 mm and the motor-bell continuous rotation envelope by about 3.106 mm.
The fixed reference motor base also clears the added guide. Four complete
9 by 2.6 mm cable-bundle gauges pass through the four wire windows.

All moving components, the motor references, original gray cover and 33 hardware
bodies retain their R2 geometry and positions. The blade-tip clearance remains
about 2.45 mm nominal. The 360 degree rear wire ring remains open and connected.
The native 39-part assembly completed 360 degrees of motion. Its interference
analysis reports only the four intentional M3-to-printed-insert pilot contacts.

PRINT AND ASSEMBLY
Use the same P1S/PLA Basic settings as the R2 nozzle: with a 0.4 mm nozzle,
0.16 mm layers and six walls are prototype starting settings. Preview supports
inside the new cup, beneath the wire slots, and around the rear guide ring.
Remove all support residue and strings from the taper, mounting face and slots.
Orient the STL before slicing; do not apply global scaling to adjust motor fit.

Route the wires through a rounded slot between the fixed guide sectors and
then through the rear ring. Lower the motor base into the taper and onto its
original flat mounting face. It should seat without force. Start all four base
screws loosely, then tighten evenly in a diagonal sequence with their washers.
Check the real motor's blind thread depths before tightening the M3x8 screws.
Install the R2 propeller and spacer as before, ensuring the hub sits level.

Hand-turn several full revolutions with the motor secured, first without and
then with the unchanged gray front cover installed. Confirm that the bell,
propeller, shaft and wires do not rub. The reference stator omits windings and
real lead details, so these physical checks remain necessary before power.
The R3 guide has not yet been printed or tested on the user's actual motor.
