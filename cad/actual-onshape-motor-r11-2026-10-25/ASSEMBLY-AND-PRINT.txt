R11 - ACTUAL ONSHAPE MOTOR AND METAL ADAPTER

Use this R11 fan with the actual adapter represented in your Motor document.
It supersedes R10's generic CPA-317-N / 6 mm-stud design. Do not mix their fans.
The metal motor, collet/threaded shaft, collar and bullet nut are native linked
copies from your saved Onshape source. They are not printed parts and have not
been resized, rebuilt or simplified. Use a physical adapter matching this CAD;
a different commercially sold A2212 adapter may have different dimensions.

What remains unchanged
The R6.1 frame, R4 cap and existing 28 mm duct extension retain their geometry
and assembly positions. No internal motor spacer, printed shaft clamp,
printed compression collar or jam ring has returned. The optional cross
mounting plate is unused because the existing frame already mounts the motor;
installing that plate in this layout would collide with the frame. Source
straight wire solids are omitted; route the flexible real wires through the
existing rear openings and keep them out of all rotating parts.

Fan fit
The stud clearance bore is 5.1 mm diameter, with a 6.2 mm diameter x 0.35 mm
rear shoulder relief. The flat hub is 7.14506 mm thick, matching the collar-to-
bullet-nut gap in your source assembly. The actual thread envelope is about
4.83 mm diameter. This is a clearance bore, not a printed thread. The collar's
broad flat flange faces the fan; its narrower tapered sleeve faces the motor.
The bullet nut's flat base faces the fan and its rounded nose faces forward.
The nut and collar clamp the hub while the collet grips the motor shaft.

Fit check before the full fan
Use the actual motor and complete matching metal adapter. The test hub must
slide onto the stud, clear the shoulder, and sit flat between the collar and
bullet nut without rocking. Confirm full collet engagement on the real shaft,
and full nut engagement. The adapter's real seating position must match the
Onshape layout; do not partially engage the shaft to obtain cap clearance.
Printer variation, shaft variants and a different adapter need a physical
fit check even when their product names are the same.

Assembly, power disconnected and cap/extension removed
1. Assemble the actual metal collet and collar on the motor shaft in the same
   orientation as the linked CAD. Follow the adapter maker's tightening and
   engagement instructions. It must clear the stationary motor body.
2. Slide the fan onto the stud, with its larger shallow bore relief facing
   the motor/collar. Seat it flat on the broad collar flange.
3. Screw on the matching bullet nut, flat base toward the fan. Hold the metal
   bell/adapter rather than the blades while tightening. Do not crush the PLA.
   Check that the fan cannot twist independently or pull off by hand.
4. Fit the motor/fan assembly into the frame from the front and route wires
   through the existing rear openings. Install all four M3x8 motor-base bolts
   with TWO 0.5 mm M3 washers beneath EACH head (eight washers total). This
   avoids bottoming in the actual source model's shallow blind holes. The CAD
   gives 2 mm nominal engagement and 0.3876 mm blind-bottom margin; check real
   screws, washers and motor holes before tightening. Tighten evenly.
5. Install the four captive cover nuts and rear washers. Fit the unchanged
   28 mm extension and cap; use the four M3x40 cover bolts with front washers.
   M3x40 bolts are longer than the photographed 6-20 mm assortment. Reuse the
   four M4 corner mounting bolt/washer/nut sets where mounting the thruster.
6. Turn by hand through several complete revolutions. Check every blade tip,
   bell, stud, nut and wire. There must be no rubbing or axial looseness.
7. Qualify with a restrained, brief low-speed run; check balance and retention
   again, including the intended direction or reversal. No powered test has
   been performed here. CAD does not guarantee collet grip, PLA creep, thrust,
   dynamic balance or water sealing.

Bambu P1S / PLA Basic starting settings
0.4 mm nozzle, 0.16 mm layers, 5 walls, 6 top/bottom layers; solid hub and bolt
bosses. Inspect blade undersides in the slicer and support where necessary.
Keep supports out of the stud bore. Remove all support residue, especially
from tips and duct surfaces. Do not scale the whole fan to fix a hole.
