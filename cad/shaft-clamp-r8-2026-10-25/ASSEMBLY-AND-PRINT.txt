WHAT CHANGED
The fan now has an integral lower shaft jaw and a removable upper jaw.
Two M3x16 socket cap screws and two ordinary M3 nuts close the jaws on the
motor axle. Four existing 5.5 mm drive keys on the 18 mm pattern still engage
the reference motor face and transmit torque. The former internal motor
spacer, external threaded compression collar and jam ring are absent.
The main frame, cap, motor references and 36 stationary fasteners retain
their original geometry and placement. Existing blade geometry is preserved.

NEW CLAMP HARDWARE (NOT PRINTED)
2 x M3x16 socket cap screws, nominal 5.68 mm head diameter, 3 mm head height.
2 x M3 hex nuts, 5.5 mm across flats, 2.4 mm thick.
Use one screw from each side, as modeled. Each has about 1.2 mm nominal
thread protrusion beyond its nut. The new clamp does not use washers.
All existing frame, cap and motor-base hardware stays as before.

ASSEMBLY ORDER
1. Leave the cap off while installing the fan. Keep the upper jaw and the
   two new screws loose/off the fan. Secure the motor base to the frame.
2. Align all four solid drive keys with the motor face pattern. Slide the
   fan onto the projecting axle until the keys are fully seated in their
   matching openings. Do not force a key into a mismatched opening.
3. Place the upper jaw in its matching rectangular seat. Its larger
   semicircular pilot relief faces the motor; the smaller shaft groove
   faces the cap/shaft tip. The stepped groove must sit around the axle.
4. Install the two M3x16 screws in opposite directions. Put a nut on each
   exposed end, outside its flat bearing pad. This matches the Onshape model.
   The 8.6 mm access wells admit an assumed 8.4 mm OD nut driver. Check your
   tool diameter; install the clamp with the cap removed.
5. Tighten alternately in small increments until the axle is gripped and
   the fan resists a firm hand pull and hand twist. Keep the keys seated.
   A small split gap must remain. If the faces close before the axle grips,
   or plastic bends/cracks, stop: the printed fit needs revision.
6. Rotate the fan by hand through a complete turn and check pull-off again.
   Then reinstall the unchanged cap with its existing through-bolts/nuts.
   Rotate by hand again with the cap fitted. Nothing should scrape or bind.

PRINT THE SMALL TEST FIRST
Print TEST-FIRST-R8-lower-jaw.stl and Upper-jaw-R8-M3-axle-clamp.stl first.
The coupon reproduces the real lower jaw's bore and clamp bolt locations
without the fan blades. Use the same printer, material and profile intended
for the complete fan. Check the actual shaft grip, jaw travel and hardware.
The coupon does not test key engagement, dynamic balance or powered thrust.

PRINT FILES / ORIENTATION
All STL dimensions are millimetres; import at 100% without scaling.
Native STL files retain the Onshape assembly coordinates. The PRINT folder
contains the same meshes rotated and placed on Z=0 for printing:
- Fan: motor-facing open cup down, motor axis vertical. Blade overhangs need
  support. Remove support carefully and smooth any blade-tip burrs.
- Upper jaw: flat shaft-tip-facing end down, axis vertical.
- Lower-jaw coupon: flat split face orientation as supplied, base at Z=0.
- Frame and cap files are unchanged reference copies; orient/support them
  as in the previous successful prints.
For P1S/PLA Basic, start with 0.16 mm layers, 6 walls and 100% upper-jaw
infill. These are starting settings, not a validated production profile.
Do not drill or sand the shaft clamp bore until checking the coupon fit.

VERIFIED CAD LIMITS
Reference shaft: diameter 3.175 mm. Unloaded bore: diameter 3.30 mm.
Available straight gripping length: 3.05 mm (Y16.25..19.30).
Nominal split gap: 0.30 mm. Geometric closing travel: about 0.125 mm.
Reference pilot: diameter 9 mm; printed relief: diameter 9.6 mm.
Conservative full-turn envelope including 0.0625 mm radial take-up allowance:
minimum frame clearance 2.3796 mm; cap clearance 0.699 mm.
Native assembly: 46 bodies, rigid rotor group of 7, one revolute mate;
Onshape interference detection: no interferences; 360-degree motion completed.
New print meshes are single closed manifold bodies.
STEP retains two microscopic inherited blade-tip sliver faces; no new edited
fan faces are invalid, and the exported fan STL is closed and manifold.

This is a testable prototype, not a guaranteed retention mechanism. The
short available grip, actual motor dimensions, PLA creep, nut loosening,
dynamic balance and operating speed/load need physical checks. Passing CAD
clearance checks cannot establish powered retention. Recheck after the first
controlled run before treating it as ready for use.
